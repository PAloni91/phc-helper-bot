ORS (oral rehydration solution): Mix one full ORS packet in one litre of clean, boiled and cooled water. Give small sips often to a person with loose motions or vomiting. Prepare fresh ORS every 24 hours and throw away what is left.

Diarrhoea warning signs: Take the person to the health centre if there is blood in the stool, if loose motions last more than 2 days, if the person cannot drink, or if a child is very sleepy, has sunken eyes, or passes very little urine.

Fever in children: Keep the child lightly dressed and give plenty of fluids. Take the child to a doctor if fever lasts more than 3 days, if the child is under 3 months old, or if there are fits, a stiff neck, fast breathing or a rash.

Emergency numbers in India: Call 108 for an ambulance or 112 for the national emergency number. Call at once for chest pain, trouble breathing, heavy bleeding, unconsciousness, fits, or snake bite.

Person not breathing and not responding: Call 108 or ask someone else to call. Lay the person on their back on a firm surface. Place the heel of your hand on the centre of the chest and push hard and fast, about 100 to 120 pushes a minute, until help arrives.

Heavy bleeding: Press firmly on the wound with a clean cloth and keep pressing. Do not remove the cloth if it soaks through; add another on top. Raise the injured limb if possible and get to a health centre quickly.

Medicines at home: Keep a small first-aid box with ORS packets, clean cotton, bandages, antiseptic solution and a thermometer. Do not give any medicine to a child or pregnant woman without asking a doctor or health worker.
